# LINAK: scans and design intent

Several scans of a motor cover provide surfaces, but not yet an unambiguous design description. Photos helped distinguish actual geometry from attached markers. Unremoved turntable geometry interfered with registration.

## From raw scan to a hypothesis

The workflow covers cleaning, registration, canonical alignment and cross-section measurements. The base form and local features are examined separately. The illustrated chart compares scan profiles with the simplified base shape at three sections.

The toolkit contains primitive fits, contours, morphology, transforms and local deviation maps. Reconstruction seeks a simple functional replacement and checks its deviations; tracing every scan irregularity without examination would not be a reliable interpretation.

## What matters in verification

Functional surfaces and local openings receive their own checks. A global average surface deviation can conceal important local errors. Sample points inside CAD triangles so that the check does not confuse tessellation structure with geometry.

The plotted curves are a development artifact. They do not certify complete or physically tested fit of the final replacement enclosure.

## Transferable rule

Every simplification is a hypothesis tied to a measurement. Photos, scans, reference frames and the functional replacement model remain distinct sources.
