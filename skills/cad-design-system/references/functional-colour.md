# Functional colour for instrument-style CAD

The user's TE research is inspiration, not a universal mechanical specification. Separate directly supplied design observations from project interpretations and uncalibrated production values. No proprietary typeface or official brand endorsement is implied.

Use a small neutral palette plus one or two accents. Assign each accent one stable function, then apply it across the part family. Keep purchased metal, belts and housings recognizable. Material contrast must distinguish a linear rail from the main plate, including in greyscale. Do not tint every fastener orange simply because it is newly added in an instruction step.

For Sparky, the established project mapping is:

| Role | Parts | Display colour |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed structure | Z base 01, permanent X carrier 09 | warm grey `#cecdc7` |
| Moving support | Wagon 02, laser adapter 04, probe arm 06 | graphite `#767977` |
| Adjustment / clamping | Slider 03, probe bracket 07, both jaws 10 | orange `#ff6116` |

These are project sRGB values, not certified RAL colours. Orange means adjustment/clamping here, not recording. Use matte physical samples to choose filament equivalents. Do not assume an AMS is fitted. Labels, part IDs, symbols and shape must carry meaning as well as colour.

Small selected facets, exposed standard hardware and aligned short labels suit this language. A grid governs new graphics and unconstrained layout; it cannot move the mechanism's actual hole pattern. Keep technical notation correct and avoid decorative scales or controls. Product design, instruction highlights and rendering style are separate layers.
